# Spaghetti + Roasted Garlic Meatballs + Butter‑Infused (Low‑Acid) Marinara

<img src="/_pics/garlic_meatball_spaghetti.jpg" alt="Spaghetti" align="left" width="25%" />

Designed to stay “classic” and not overly complex for a picky eater, while
still tasting rich and comforting for everyone else

For this dish, I wanted a Spaghetti my daughter would like (who is both a picky
eater and has a sensitive stomach) but also something with some flavor and nuance
that my wife and I would enjoy.

The marinara sauce is butter-infused to reduce acidity, and the meatballs are
made with a decedant roasted garlic mix that is to die for.

**Yield + timing**:

* Serves: 4–6 (about 16–20 meatballs depending on size)
* Total time: ~1 hour 10 minutes (you can overlap steps)
* Active time: ~25–30 minutes

## Ingredients

### A) Roast garlic (for the meatballs)

* 1 whole head of garlic
    - Any garlic will do, but if you can find a jumbo garlic, that's the way to go
* 1-2 tsp olive oil
* Pinch of salt
* Foil (or a small baking dish with a lid)

### B) Butter‑infused marinara (mellow, not sharp)

* 2 cans (28 oz each) whole peeled tomatoes (or crushed, but whole are often smoother/less sharp after simmering)
* 1 yellow onion, peeled and cut in half (keep halves intact)
    - A nice variant here is shallots. Slice them or cut them into chunks. The peeled onion will come out, but you can leave the shallots in.
* 5 Tbsp unsalted butter
* 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
* Optional (choose based on stomach tolerance):
    - 1 small carrot, peeled (added whole to simmer for sweetness; remove at end)
    - Pinch of baking soda (start with 1/16-1/8 tsp) if tomatoes still taste acidic
    - 1 small Parmesan rind (adds savory depth without “spices”)

### C) Roasted garlic beef meatballs

* 1 ½ lb ground beef (80/20 is ideal for tenderness)
* ½ cup plain breadcrumbs (or 1-2 slices soft white bread, torn up)
* ⅓ cup milk (any kind; helps tenderness)
* 1 egg
* ½ cup finely grated Parmesan
    - A fun alternative is some finly grated cotija
* Roasted garlic from 1 head (see above), mashed into a paste
* 1-1 ¼ tsp kosher salt
* ½ tsp black pepper (optional if picky eater is sensitive)
* 2 Tbsp finely chopped parsley (optional; mild)
* 1-2 Tbsp olive oil (only if pan-browning; not needed if baking)

### D) Pasta + finishing

* 1 lb spaghetti
* Salt for pasta water (it should taste pleasantly salty)
* More Parmesan for serving
    - A fun alternative is some finly grated cotija
* Optional “fun for others” at the table: (served separately)
    - torn basil
    - chili flakes
    - extra black pepper

## Step-by-step instructions (with timing)

### 1) Roast the garlic (40-50 minutes, mostly hands-off)

1. Heat oven to 400°F (205°C).
2. Slice the top off the garlic head (just enough to expose the cloves).
3. Drizzle with olive oil, sprinkle a pinch of salt, wrap in foil.
    - The ideal here is to have a garlic head that forms a "cup" for the oil
      to reside.
4. Roast 40-45 minutes until cloves are very soft and golden.
5. Let cool 5-10 minutes, then squeeze cloves out and mash into a paste.

> **Overlap tip**: While the garlic roasts, start the sauce (Step 2).

### 2) Make the butter‑infused marinara (35–50 minutes)

1. In a medium/large saucepan, add:
    - Tomatoes (with their juice)
    - Butter
    - Onion halves (or sliced/chunked shallots)
    - Salt
    - **Optional**: whole carrot and/or Parmesan rind
2. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, then reduce to low so it “blips” quietly.
3. Simmer 35-45 minutes, stirring occasionally and crushing the tomatoes with a spoon as they soften.
4. Remove the onion halves (and carrot/rind if used).
    - If you used sliced/chunked shallots, they should have fallen apart by now and will be too hard to remove. Just leave them in, they will be a soft and delicious bonus.
5. Taste:
    - If it still tastes acidic/sharp, add a tiny pinch of baking soda (start 1/16 tsp), stir well, simmer 2 minutes, then taste again. Don’t overdo it; too much can taste flat/soapy.

> **Optional**: For a smoother picky-eater-friendly sauce, use an immersion blender briefly, or blend a portion.

### 3) Mix and shape meatballs (10–15 minutes)

1. Make the panade:
    1. In a bowl, mix breadcrumbs + milk. Let sit 2-3 minutes to absorb.
    2. Add egg, Parmesan, mashed roasted garlic, salt, (pepper/parsley if using). Mix.
2. Add ground beef and gently mix just until combined (overmixing = tough meatballs).
3. Shape into 16-20 meatballs (about 1½ inches / golf-ball size).

### 4) Cook the meatballs (choose one)
#### Option A (easiest/cleanest): Bake (18–22 minutes)

1. Heat oven to 425°F (220°C).
2. Place meatballs on a parchment-lined sheet pan.
3. Bake 18-22 minutes until browned and cooked through (160°F/71°C internal).
4. Transfer to the simmering sauce and let them meld 10 minutes on low.

#### Option B (best browning): Pan-brown then simmer (20–25 minutes)

1. Heat 1-2 Tbsp olive oil in a large skillet over medium.
2. Brown meatballs in batches 6-8 minutes total, turning to brown several sides (they don’t need to be cooked through yet).
3. Move browned meatballs into the sauce; simmer gently 10-15 minutes until cooked through.

> If stomach issues flare with heavy browning, baking + a gentle simmer is often easier to tolerate.

### 5) Cook spaghetti + combine (10–12 minutes)

1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Salt it well.
2. Cook spaghetti until al dente (check the package time; usually 8-11 minutes).
3. Reserve ½ cup pasta water, then drain.
4. Toss spaghetti with a few ladles of sauce (add a splash of pasta water if you want it silkier).
5. Serve with meatballs on top and extra sauce.

# Serving suggestions (picky-eater friendly + “fun” for others)

* Keep the main bowl plain and classic.
* Put add-ons in small bowls so others can customize:
    - Basil or parsley
    - Chili flakes
    - Extra Parmesan or cotija
    - A drizzle of good olive oil
