## The Classic Herbaceous Blend

This is the traditional "New York Style" profile that relies heavily on dried herbs for that nostalgic pizzeria aroma. Dried herbs often work better than fresh in pizza sauce because they stand up to the high heat of the oven without turning bitter.

* **Dried Oregano**: 1 teaspoon (the most essential pizza herb)
* **Dried Basil**: 1/2 teaspoon
* **Garlic Powder**: 1/2 teaspoon (provides a more uniform flavor than fresh)
* **Onion Powder**: 1/4 teaspoon
* **Pinch of Sugar**: To cut the acidity of the tomatoes

## The Spicy Garlic Kick

If you prefer a sauce with more depth and a lingering heat, this combo focuses on savory aromatics and red pepper. It works particularly well with salty toppings like pepperoni or sausage.

* **Fresh Minced Garlic**: 2 cloves (sautéed briefly in olive oil before adding the sauce)
* **Red Pepper Flakes**: 1/2 to 1 teaspoon (depending on your heat tolerance)
* **Dried Thyme**: 1/4 teaspoon
* **Black Pepper**: 1/2 teaspoon freshly cracked
* **Splash of Balsamic Vinegar**: For a deep, tangy finish

## The Savory Umami Boost

This combination adds layers of "meatiness" and richness to the sauce without adding actual meat, making the tomato flavor taste more "expensive" and complex.

* **Fennel Seeds**: 1/2 teaspoon (crushed or toasted; this is the "secret" ingredient in many Italian sausages)
* **Smoked Paprika**: 1/4 teaspoon (adds a subtle wood-fired depth)
* **Grated Parmesan Cheese**: 1 tablespoon stirred directly into the sauce
* **Dried Marjoram**: 1/2 teaspoon (a sweeter, more delicate cousin to oregano)
* **Extra Virgin Olive Oil**: 1 tablespoon swirled in at the end for a silky mouthfeel

For all of these, I recommend letting the sauce sit for at least 30 minutes after mixing so the dried spices have time to rehydrate and infuse the tomato base. If your sauce feels too thin, you can simmer it on low heat for 10-15 minutes to thicken it up so it doesn't make your pizza crust soggy.
